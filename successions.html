When a loved one passes away, their property does not transfer to family members automatically. In Louisiana, that transfer happens through a court-supervised legal process called a succession. Whether you are administering a parent’s estate or planning ahead for your own family, working with an experienced succession attorney helps ensure assets are handled correctly and according to the law. What Is a Succession in Louisiana?

A succession — what most other states call probate — is the legal process of transferring a deceased person’s assets, property, and debts to their heirs or legatees. The process involves identifying the estate’s assets and debts, determining the rightful heirs, paying any valid creditor claims, and ultimately placing the heirs in possession of the property. Because Louisiana follows a civil-law tradition with rules that differ from the rest of the country — including community property and forced heirship — having local guidance matters.

Testate vs. Intestate Successions

How an estate is handled depends largely on whether the deceased left a valid will. A testate succession occurs when there is a valid will (testament) directing how the property should be distributed. The named executor carries out those wishes, subject to Louisiana law, including the rights of any forced heirs. An intestate succession occurs when someone dies without a valid will. In that case, Louisiana’s intestacy laws decide who inherits, generally favoring children, spouses, and other close relatives in a set order. Intestate successions can become complicated, especially when community property and separate property are involved, which is why legal guidance is so valuable.

Small Successions

Not every estate requires a full, formal court proceeding. Louisiana law provides for a simplified “small succession” procedure for certain estates that fall under a statutory value threshold, or when a person has been deceased for a number of years. A small succession can often be handled by affidavit rather than a full court administration, saving the family time and expense. Chris can review the estate to determine whether it qualifies for this streamlined option.

The Succession Process

While every estate is different, most successions follow a similar path:

  • Identify the assets and debts. Gather information on bank accounts, real estate, vehicles, investments, and any outstanding obligations of the estate.
  • Determine the heirs. Confirm whether there is a valid will and identify the heirs or legatees entitled to inherit under the will or Louisiana law.
  • File the succession. Prepare and file the petition and supporting documents with the appropriate court, including a detailed descriptive list of the estate.
  • Administer and pay claims. Address valid creditor claims, taxes, and administrative matters before distributing property.
  • Place heirs in possession. The court issues a judgment of possession transferring ownership of the assets to the rightful heirs.
