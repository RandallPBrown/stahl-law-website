Drug Crime Defense Attorney in Bossier City

Defense against possession, distribution, and trafficking charges throughout Bossier City and Shreveport.

A drug charge in Louisiana can change your life. Depending on the substance, the amount, and what the State alleges you intended to do with it, you could be facing anything from probation to many years in prison — along with fines and a criminal record that makes it harder to find work or housing. If you have been arrested for a drug offense in Bossier City or Shreveport, the most important step you can take is to speak with an experienced criminal defense attorney like Christopher Stahl before you say anything to investigators.

How Louisiana Classifies Controlled Substances

Louisiana’s Uniform Controlled Dangerous Substances Law, found in Title 40 of the Louisiana Revised Statutes (La. R.S. 40:961 and following), organizes drugs into five “schedules.” The schedule reflects how dangerous the State considers a substance and whether it has any accepted medical use:

  • Schedule I: Substances treated as having a high potential for abuse and no accepted medical use, such as heroin.
  • Schedule II: High potential for abuse but with some accepted medical use, including cocaine, methamphetamine, and many opioid painkillers.
  • Schedule III, IV, and V: Substances with progressively lower potential for abuse, including many prescription medications.

The schedule of the drug, together with the quantity and the alleged conduct, drives how serious the charge is and what penalties apply.

Possession vs. Distribution vs. Trafficking

One of the most important things to understand about Louisiana drug law is that the same substance can lead to very different charges depending on what the State believes you were doing with it.

Possession (La. R.S. 40:966 and following)

Simple possession means having a controlled substance for personal use. Penalties depend heavily on the schedule and amount. Possession of a small amount of certain substances may be charged as a lower-level offense, while possession of Schedule I or II drugs such as heroin, cocaine, or methamphetamine can carry significant prison exposure. Even possession of marijuana is governed by Louisiana law, with penalties that depend on the amount involved and any prior offenses.

Possession With Intent to Distribute

Prosecutors do not have to catch someone in the act of selling to charge distribution. “Possession with intent to distribute” is a far more serious charge than simple possession, and the State often tries to prove intent through circumstantial evidence — the quantity of the drug, packaging in multiple small bags, scales, large amounts of cash, or text messages. A skilled defense attorney examines whether that evidence truly supports an inference of intent, or whether the facts are more consistent with personal possession.

Distribution and Trafficking

Distribution covers selling, delivering, or otherwise transferring a controlled substance. Trafficking-level offenses generally involve larger quantities and carry Louisiana’s harshest drug penalties, sometimes including mandatory minimum sentences. The specific exposure depends on the substance and the weight involved, which is why the precise charge and the State’s evidence about quantity matter so much.

Other Common Drug Charges

  • Possession of drug paraphernalia: Items used to consume, store, or prepare controlled substances.
  • Prescription fraud: Obtaining medication through forged prescriptions or misrepresentation.
  • Enhanced charges: Louisiana provides for increased penalties for offenses committed in certain locations, such as near a school or playground.

How Drug Cases Are Defended

Many drug cases turn on how the evidence was obtained. The Fourth Amendment protects you against unreasonable searches and seizures, and if police searched your car, home, or person without a valid warrant or a recognized exception, the evidence may be subject to suppression. An attorney can also challenge whether the substance was actually a controlled dangerous substance, whether the State can prove you knowingly possessed it, and whether the chain of custody and lab testing were handled properly. Defending a charge can mean filing motions to suppress, negotiating a reduced charge, or taking the case to trial.

Diversion and Drug Court in Louisiana

Not every drug case has to end in a conviction. Louisiana law allows for pretrial diversion programs and specialized drug courts in many jurisdictions, including the Bossier City and Shreveport area. These programs focus on treatment, supervision, and accountability rather than incarceration, and successful completion can result in a dismissal of charges — which in turn may make a later expungement possible. Eligibility depends on the offense and your history, and an attorney can advise whether diversion or drug court is a realistic option in your case. For first-time offenders especially, this path can protect your record and your future.
