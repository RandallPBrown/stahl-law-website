Defending Against a Drug Trafficking Charge in Louisiana

By Christopher M. Stahl · Criminal Defense

Few accusations carry higher stakes than drug trafficking. In Louisiana, these are serious felony charges that can mean years in prison, steep fines, and a permanent mark on your record. If you or someone you love is facing such a charge in the Shreveport–Bossier City area, understanding how the law works — and how a defense is built — can make a meaningful difference. This article explains the basics and the strategies a defense attorney uses to challenge the State’s case.

What Is Drug Trafficking?

Broadly, drug trafficking involves the manufacture, distribution, or possession with intent to distribute a controlled dangerous substance. In Louisiana, these offenses are governed primarily by the Uniform Controlled Dangerous Substances Law, including La. R.S. 40:966 et seq., which sets out the crimes and penalties for substances ranging from marijuana to narcotics like heroin, cocaine, and methamphetamine.

Two points are important. First, prosecutors must prove more than mere presence near drugs — for a possession-with-intent or distribution charge, the State has to establish that you knowingly possessed the substance and intended to distribute it, often inferred from quantity, packaging, scales, cash, or other circumstances. Second, drug offenses can be charged under state law in Louisiana, or under federal law — particularly when conduct is alleged to cross state lines or involve large quantities. The forum matters, because penalties and procedures differ.

Penalties Can Be Severe

Louisiana grades drug penalties by the type and schedule of the substance and the amount involved. Higher schedules and larger quantities trigger harsher sentences, and certain weights carry mandatory minimum prison terms and substantial fines. Aggravating factors — such as alleged distribution near a school or to a minor — can increase the exposure further. Because the consequences are so serious, every element of the State’s case deserves scrutiny.

How a Drug Trafficking Charge Can Be Challenged

A strong defense begins long before trial. Some of the most common avenues include:

  • Challenging the search and seizure. The Fourth Amendment protects you from unreasonable searches. If law enforcement searched your person, vehicle, or home without a valid warrant or a recognized exception — or without probable cause — evidence obtained may be suppressed. When key evidence is thrown out, the prosecution’s case can collapse.
  • Contesting possession and intent. The State must tie the substance to you and prove you intended to distribute it. Questions about who actually controlled the drugs, whether the quantity truly indicates distribution, and the reliability of any informant can all undermine the charge.
  • Scrutinizing the lab and the chain of custody. The identity and weight of the alleged substance must be properly tested and documented. Gaps in the chain of custody or flaws in testing can weaken the evidence.
  • Protecting your statements. Anything you say can be used against you. If you were questioned without proper advisement of your rights, statements may be challenged.

Protect Yourself From the Start

The single most important thing to remember is that you have the right to remain silent and the right to an attorney. If you are stopped or arrested, you are not required to answer questions, and you should not try to talk your way out of the situation — doing so often does more harm than good. Politely state that you wish to speak with a lawyer, and then say nothing further about the case.

It is also wise to retain counsel as early as possible. Hiring an attorney soon after arrest — ideally before any indictment — gives your defense time to investigate, preserve evidence, file motions, and develop a strategy. Waiting until the case is already moving forward can cost you valuable preparation time.

Why Experienced Local Counsel Matters

Drug trafficking cases are document- and procedure-intensive, and the difference between a dismissal, a reduced charge, and a conviction often lies in the details — the legality of the stop, the strength of the lab work, and the persuasiveness of the defense presented. A criminal defense attorney who practices in Louisiana courts can evaluate the evidence against you, identify constitutional issues, and fight to protect your rights and your future.
