A dog bite can cause far more than a painful wound. Victims often face permanent scarring, nerve damage, infection, costly medical care, and lasting emotional trauma — and children are especially vulnerable. Each year, people across Shreveport and Bossier City are seriously injured by dogs whose owners failed to control them. If you or your child was bitten, the Law Office of Christopher M. Louisiana Dog Bite Law

Louisiana is not a traditional “one-bite” state. Liability for a dog attack is governed by Louisiana Civil Code article 2321, which provides that the owner of an animal is responsible for the damage it causes. For dogs specifically, the law imposes what amounts to a strict-liability standard: an owner is liable for damages caused by their dog when the owner knew or should have known the dog’s behavior would cause damage, the damage could have been prevented by reasonable care, and the owner failed to exercise that care. Importantly, the injured person does not have to prove the dog had bitten anyone before — the focus is on whether the harm was preventable.

Provocation and Trespassing

An owner may raise certain defenses. If the victim provoked the dog or was trespassing or otherwise somewhere they had no right to be, the owner’s responsibility may be reduced or eliminated. Whether conduct counts as “provocation” is fact-specific, which is one reason a careful investigation matters.

Dangerous Dog Rules and Leash Laws

Louisiana also has dangerous-dog provisions and local leash ordinances that vary by parish and city. A dog that has bitten or attacked a person or another animal without provocation may be classified as dangerous, triggering registration, confinement, leashing, and muzzling requirements. A violation of a leash law or dangerous-dog order can be strong evidence that the owner failed to use reasonable care.

Who Can Be Held Liable

In most cases the dog’s owner is the responsible party, and their homeowner’s or renter’s insurance often provides coverage for the claim. In some situations, others may share responsibility as well:

  • Landlords, if they knew a tenant’s dog was dangerous and had the ability to remove or restrain it but failed to act.
  • Property owners or keepers who allowed a dangerous animal on premises they controlled.
  • Caretakers or handlers who had custody of the dog at the time of the attack.

Identifying every potentially liable party — and the insurance that may cover your losses — is an important part of building your claim.

Common Dog Bite Injuries

Dog attacks can cause severe and lasting harm, including:

  • Puncture wounds and deep lacerations requiring stitches or surgery.
  • Infections, including rabies risk and serious bacterial infections.
  • Nerve and muscle damage that can impair function permanently.
  • Permanent scarring and disfigurement, often requiring reconstructive surgery.
  • Broken bones from being knocked down or dragged.
  • Emotional trauma, including anxiety, PTSD, and a lasting fear of dogs.

Damages You May Recover

Through a dog bite claim, an injured person may be able to recover compensation for both economic and non-economic losses, such as:

  • Medical expenses — emergency care, surgery, reconstructive procedures, and follow-up treatment.
  • Lost wages and lost earning capacity if the injury keeps you from working.
  • Pain and suffering for the physical pain and ongoing limitations.
  • Emotional distress, including anxiety, depression, and post-traumatic stress.
  • Scarring and disfigurement, particularly important in facial injuries.

Because Louisiana follows a pure comparative fault rule under La. C.C. art. 2323, any compensation may be reduced by the percentage of fault attributed to the injured person, but is not automatically barred.

The One-Year Deadline to File

Louisiana sets a short deadline for injury claims. Under La. C.C. art. 3492, you generally have only one year from the date of the bite to file a personal injury lawsuit. If you wait too long, you can lose the right to recover entirely. Speaking with an attorney promptly also helps preserve evidence such as the dog’s history, animal-control records, and witness accounts.

What to Do After a Dog Bite

If you are bitten, seek medical attention right away — even seemingly minor bites can become infected. Report the attack to local animal control, which creates an official record and may document the dog’s history. Get the owner’s name and contact information, photograph your injuries and the location, and collect the names of any witnesses. Then contact an attorney before discussing the incident or any settlement with the owner’s insurance company.
