What Is a DWI in Louisiana?

In Louisiana, “DWI” means driving while intoxicated and “DUI” means driving under the influence. The terms are often used interchangeably, and under Louisiana’s operating-while-intoxicated statute (La. R.S. 14:98) they describe the same offense: operating a motor vehicle, aircraft, or watercraft while impaired. You can be charged with a DWI in Louisiana if you operate a vehicle:

  • With a blood alcohol concentration (BAC) of 0.08% or greater; or
  • While under the influence of alcohol, illegal drugs, legally prescribed medication, or any combination of these.

The legal limit is lower for some drivers. Commercial drivers can be charged at a BAC of 0.04%, and for drivers under 21 Louisiana’s underage law applies at a BAC as low as 0.02%. Even if you refuse testing, an officer can still arrest you if there is reasonable cause to believe you are intoxicated.

Implied Consent and Sobriety Tests

By driving on Louisiana roads, you give “implied consent” to chemical testing of your breath, blood, or urine if you are lawfully arrested for DWI. You can physically refuse a breath or blood test, but refusal carries its own consequences: your driving privileges can be suspended, and your refusal can be used against you. Field sobriety tests — the horizontal gaze nystagmus (eye) test, the walk-and-turn test, and the one-leg-stand test — are different. They are voluntary, they are frequently inaccurate, and many medical conditions can cause an innocent person to “fail.” An experienced attorney can challenge how these tests were given and what they actually prove.

Louisiana DWI Penalties by Offense

The penalties for a DWI in Louisiana depend heavily on how many prior convictions are on your record within the relevant “cleansing period.” Even a first offense can be costly.

First Offense

  • Jail: Up to six months. In practice, a short jail term or probation is far more common for a first offense.
  • Fines: Generally between $300 and $1,000, plus court costs.
  • Classes and community service: Substance-abuse and driver-improvement programs, plus community service hours.
  • Insurance: You may be required to carry costly SR-22 insurance, which can raise your premiums significantly.

Second Offense

A second DWI within the cleansing period brings a minimum jail term (a portion of which must be served without suspension or probation), fines generally between $750 and $1,000, hundreds of hours of community service, mandatory education, an ignition-interlock device, and a one-year license suspension.

Third Offense

A third DWI is a felony in Louisiana. Penalties escalate sharply — substantial mandatory prison time, fines around $2,000, an ignition-interlock requirement, possible seizure of your vehicle, and revocation of your driver’s license. A fourth offense is treated even more harshly. Because penalties compound with each conviction, fighting even a first charge matters.

License Suspension After a DWI Arrest

A DWI in Louisiana creates two separate problems: the criminal case in court and an administrative action against your driver’s license through the Office of Motor Vehicles. These proceedings move on different tracks and different deadlines. You generally have only a short window after your arrest to request an administrative hearing to contest the suspension of your license, so it is important to act quickly. An attorney can help you protect your driving privileges while also defending the criminal charge.

What to Do After a DWI Arrest

  • Be polite, but do not volunteer statements about how much you had to drink.
  • Write down everything you remember — the stop, the questions, and the testing — while it is fresh.
  • Note the deadline to request a license hearing and do not let it pass.
  • Contact a DWI defense attorney before your first court date so your rights are protected from the start.

Can a DWI Charge Be Fought?

Yes. A DWI charge is not a conviction, and there are real ways to challenge one. An attorney may question whether the officer had a lawful reason to stop you, whether there was probable cause for the arrest, whether the breath-testing instrument was properly maintained and calibrated, and whether the field sobriety tests were administered correctly. The goals are to defend the charge and seek a not-guilty result, to minimize jail time and other penalties, and where possible to keep a DWI off your record.
