What to Do After a Car Accident in Bossier City

By Christopher M. Stahl · June 3, 2026

What to Do After a Car Accident in Bossier City, Louisiana

A car accident can turn an ordinary day into one of the most stressful experiences of your life. Whether it happens on Barksdale Boulevard, the Texas Street Bridge, or anywhere else in the Bossier City and Shreveport area, the moments after a crash are critical. Knowing what to do — and what not to do — can make a significant difference in your health, your finances, and any future legal claim you may have.

This article provides general legal information to help you navigate those first steps. It is not a substitute for personalized legal advice from a licensed Louisiana attorney.

Step 1: Check for Injuries and Call 911

Your safety and the safety of everyone involved is the top priority. Before anything else, check yourself and your passengers for injuries. Even if you feel fine, some injuries — like whiplash, concussions, or internal trauma — may not be immediately obvious.

Call 911 right away. A police response is important for several reasons:

  • Officers will document the scene and create an official accident report.
  • Emergency medical personnel can assess injuries on the spot.
  • An official report from the Bossier City Police Department or the Bossier Parish Sheriff's Office creates a formal record that is valuable for any insurance or legal claim.

If it is safe to do so, move vehicles out of traffic to prevent further collisions. Turn on hazard lights and use road flares or cones if available.

Step 2: Exchange Information at the Scene

While waiting for officers to arrive, exchange the following information with all other drivers involved:

  • Full name and contact information
  • Driver's license number
  • Vehicle registration and license plate number
  • Insurance company name and policy number

If there are witnesses, kindly ask for their names and phone numbers as well. Witness accounts can be extremely valuable if the facts of the accident are later disputed.

Step 3: Document Everything You Can

Your smartphone is one of the most useful tools you have at an accident scene. Take photos and videos of:

  • All vehicle damage, from multiple angles
  • The overall accident scene, including road conditions, traffic signs, and skid marks
  • Any visible injuries
  • The positions of the vehicles before they are moved

The more documentation you have, the better. Evidence gathered in those first minutes can be difficult or impossible to recreate later.

Step 4: Seek Medical Attention Promptly

Even if you walked away from the crash feeling relatively okay, see a doctor as soon as possible — ideally the same day. Adrenaline and shock can mask pain. Conditions like soft-tissue injuries, herniated discs, or mild traumatic brain injuries may not fully present themselves until hours or days after the accident.

Getting timely medical care is not just important for your health; it also creates a documented medical record linking your injuries to the accident. Delays in seeking treatment can be used by insurance companies to argue that your injuries were not serious or were caused by something else entirely.

Step 5: Do Not Admit Fault

This is one of the most important rules to follow. At the scene, avoid saying things like "I'm sorry" or "I didn't see you" — even as a reflexive, polite gesture. Statements like these can be interpreted as an admission of liability and used against you later.

Stick to the facts when speaking with police. Louisiana follows a comparative fault system, meaning that fault can be apportioned among multiple parties. What you say in those moments can affect how fault is ultimately assigned and how much compensation you may be entitled to receive.

Step 6: Call an Attorney Before Talking to the Other Driver's Insurance

After a crash, you can expect to hear from the other driver's insurance company. Their adjusters are experienced professionals whose job is to settle claims — often as quickly and inexpensively as possible. You are not required to give a recorded statement to the other party's insurer, and doing so without legal guidance can seriously harm your case.

Before you speak with any insurance representative other than your own, consult with a Louisiana personal injury attorney. An attorney can help you understand your rights under Louisiana law, evaluate the true value of your claim, and handle communications with the insurer on your behalf.

Louisiana Has Time Limits — Don't Wait Too Long

Louisiana law sets a deadline — known as a prescriptive period — for filing personal injury claims arising from car accidents. Missing this deadline can permanently bar you from seeking compensation, no matter how strong your case may be. The sooner you speak with an attorney, the more time there is to properly investigate, gather evidence, and build your claim.
