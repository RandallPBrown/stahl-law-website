What to Do After a Car Accident in Bossier City

By Christopher M. Stahl · June 3, 2026

What to Do After a Car Accident in Bossier City

Car accidents happen in an instant, but the decisions you make in the minutes, hours, and days afterward can have a lasting impact on your health, your finances, and your legal rights. If you or a loved one has been involved in a crash on Interstate 20, the Texas Street Bridge, or anywhere else in the Bossier City and Shreveport area, here is a clear, step-by-step guide to help you navigate what comes next.

1. Check for Injuries and Call 911 Immediately

Your safety — and the safety of everyone involved — comes first. Before anything else, check yourself and your passengers for injuries. Even if injuries seem minor, call 911 right away. Louisiana law requires drivers involved in accidents that result in injury, death, or significant property damage to report the crash to law enforcement.

When officers arrive, they will document the scene and generate a police report. That report can become a critical piece of evidence if you later need to file an insurance claim or pursue legal action. Make sure you obtain the report number before you leave.

2. Exchange Information With the Other Driver

While you wait for law enforcement, exchange the following information with all other drivers involved:

  • Full name and contact information
  • Driver's license number
  • License plate number
  • Insurance company name and policy number
  • Vehicle make, model, and year

If there are witnesses nearby — whether at a gas station, a nearby business, or simply passing by — kindly ask for their names and phone numbers as well. Witness accounts can make a real difference when liability is disputed.

3. Document the Scene Thoroughly

Modern smartphones make it easier than ever to preserve evidence. Take photos and video of everything you can, including:

  • All vehicles involved, from multiple angles
  • Visible damage to each vehicle
  • Skid marks, debris, or road conditions
  • Traffic signs, signals, and the surrounding intersection or roadway
  • Any visible injuries you or your passengers have sustained

The more documentation you have from the scene, the stronger your position will be later. Do not assume the insurance company or police will gather all of this for you.

4. Seek Medical Attention Promptly — Even If You Feel Fine

One of the most important things you can do after any accident is see a doctor as soon as possible, even if you do not feel seriously hurt. Many common injuries — such as whiplash, soft tissue damage, and even concussions — do not produce obvious symptoms right away. Adrenaline can mask pain for hours or even days after a crash.

Seeking prompt medical care does two important things: it protects your health, and it creates a medical record that documents your injuries and connects them to the accident. Under Louisiana law, delays in seeking treatment can be used by insurance companies to argue that your injuries were not caused by the crash or were not serious. Do not give them that opening.

5. Do Not Admit Fault at the Scene

In the aftermath of a stressful collision, it is natural to want to apologize or say something reassuring — but be very careful with your words. Do not admit fault, speculate about what happened, or make any statements that could be interpreted as accepting blame. Even a simple "I'm sorry" can be used against you later.

Stick to the facts when speaking with law enforcement, and let the investigation determine what happened. Louisiana follows a comparative fault system, meaning that fault can be shared between multiple parties, and any admission you make could reduce or eliminate your ability to recover compensation.

6. Call an Attorney Before You Talk to the Other Driver's Insurance Company

You will likely hear from the other driver's insurance company quickly — sometimes within hours of an accident. Their adjusters are experienced professionals whose job is to settle claims for as little money as possible. You are under no obligation to give a recorded statement or accept any settlement offer before speaking with an attorney.

An experienced Louisiana personal injury attorney can evaluate your claim, communicate with insurers on your behalf, and help ensure that any settlement reflects the true value of your damages — including medical bills, lost wages, vehicle repair, and pain and suffering. Consulting with an attorney early in the process costs you nothing, but it can make an enormous difference in the outcome of your case.
