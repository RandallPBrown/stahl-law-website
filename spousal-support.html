Factors That Affect Spousal Support in Louisiana

By Christopher M. Stahl · Family Law

When a marriage ends, the question of spousal support is often one of the most contested issues in the case. Whether you expect to pay support or to receive it, understanding the factors a Louisiana court weighs will help you set realistic expectations and prepare. This article breaks down the types of support, what qualifies someone to receive it, and how courts evaluate awards.

Types of Spousal Support in Louisiana

Louisiana law recognizes two main forms of spousal support, and it is important not to confuse them:

  • Interim spousal support is temporary assistance paid while the divorce is pending. Its purpose is to help maintain the financial status quo and bridge the gap until the divorce is final. It is based largely on need and the other spouse’s ability to pay.
  • Final periodic support may be awarded after the divorce when one spouse demonstrates a real need and the other has the means to pay. Depending on the circumstances, it can be time-limited (sometimes called rehabilitative, to help a spouse become self-supporting) or, in limited cases, longer-term.

Support is most often paid in periodic installments, though in some situations a lump-sum arrangement may be appropriate. The judge sets the form, amount, and duration based on the facts of the case.

What Qualifies a Spouse to Receive Support

Before awarding final support, the court must find an actual need. It does this by examining each spouse’s income, financial resources, and reasonable living expenses — including the cost of necessities such as housing and food. The court also considers the impact the divorce has on the income of the spouse who may receive support.

A distinctive feature of Louisiana law is the role of fault. Generally, a spouse who is found to have been at fault in causing the breakup of the marriage may be barred from receiving final periodic support. This makes the conduct of each spouse a potentially decisive issue, and it is one reason experienced representation matters.

How Courts Evaluate the Amount and Duration

To set an award, the court must confirm two things: the receiving spouse’s need for assistance and the paying spouse’s ability to provide it. Beyond those core questions, courts in Louisiana consider a range of factors, which commonly include:

  • The income, means, and earning capacity of each spouse;
  • The financial obligations of each spouse;
  • The age and health of the parties;
  • The duration of the marriage;
  • The time needed for the recipient to acquire education or training to find employment;
  • The effect of custody of children on a party’s earning capacity; and
  • The tax consequences to each spouse.

By statute, a final periodic support award generally may not exceed one-third (about 33%) of the paying spouse’s net income. If you have children, the different forms of custody can also factor into the support analysis, because custody arrangements affect each parent’s earning capacity and expenses.

Modifying or Ending Support

Spousal support is not always permanent or fixed. If circumstances change materially, either spouse can ask the court to modify the award by filing a petition that explains the reason for the requested change in amount or duration. Support also typically terminates upon events such as the remarriage of the recipient or the death of either party, and only the court can stop court-ordered payments while the obligation remains in effect.

Why Guidance Matters

Because spousal support turns on detailed financial proof and, in many cases, on questions of fault, the outcome can vary widely depending on how the case is presented. Carefully documenting income, expenses, and the circumstances of the marriage — and understanding the statutory limits — can make a significant difference in the result. Remember, too, that filing a petition with the court involves a filing fee.
