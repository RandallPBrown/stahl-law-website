18-Wheeler Accident Attorney in Shreveport

Truck crashes cause devastating injuries and involve powerful companies and insurers — we level the field, with no fee unless we win.

Shreveport sits at the crossroads of Interstates 20 and 49, and the heavy flow of commercial trucks through Northwest Louisiana means 18-wheeler crashes are an all-too-common reality. A fully loaded tractor-trailer can weigh 80,000 pounds — roughly twenty times the weight of a passenger car — so when one collides with a smaller vehicle, the results are often catastrophic. If you or someone you love was hurt in a truck accident, the Law Office of Christopher M. Stahl can help you navigate these complex cases and pursue the compensation you deserve.

Why Truck Cases Are Different

An 18-wheeler accident is not simply a bigger car accident. These claims differ in several important ways that make experienced representation essential:

  • Severity of injuries. The size and weight of commercial trucks produce far more force on impact, leading to traumatic brain injuries, spinal cord damage, amputations, and fatalities.
  • Layers of regulation. The trucking industry is governed by detailed federal and state safety rules that ordinary drivers never face.
  • Multiple potentially liable parties. More than one company may share responsibility for a single crash.
  • Aggressive defense. Trucking companies and their insurers often send investigators to the scene within hours, working to limit their liability from the start.

Federal Trucking Regulations

Commercial carriers and their drivers must follow the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations enforced by the FMCSA. These rules cover hours-of-service limits designed to prevent fatigue, mandatory rest breaks, driver qualification and licensing, drug and alcohol testing, vehicle inspection and maintenance, and limits on how cargo is loaded and secured. When a trucking company or driver violates one of these regulations and a crash results, that violation can be powerful evidence of negligence. Records such as electronic logging device (ELD) data, driver logs, and maintenance files often reveal whether the rules were broken — but they must be requested before they disappear.

Common Causes of 18-Wheeler Accidents

Many truck crashes trace back to preventable conduct, including:

  • Driver fatigue from long hours and pressure to meet tight delivery deadlines.
  • Distracted or reckless driving, including speeding, tailgating, and unsafe lane changes.
  • Improperly loaded or overweight cargo that affects braking and stability.
  • Inadequate maintenance leading to brake failures and tire blowouts.
  • Impaired driving from alcohol, drugs, or even some medications.
  • Poor weather and reduced visibility that a careful professional driver should account for.

Multiple Parties May Be Liable

One of the biggest differences in a truck case is that responsibility often extends well beyond the driver. Depending on the facts, liable parties may include the truck driver, the trucking company that employed and supervised the driver, the owner of the trailer or tractor, the company that loaded the cargo, a maintenance contractor, or even the manufacturer of a defective part. Trucking companies frequently carry large insurance policies, but they also have legal teams whose job is to minimize what they pay. Identifying every responsible party is critical to recovering the full compensation you are owed.

Preserving the Evidence

Critical evidence in a truck case can vanish quickly. Logbooks may be overwritten, the truck may be repaired or returned to service, and the “black box” data recorder may be reset. Acting fast lets your attorney send a spoliation (evidence-preservation) letter demanding that the company retain key records, and gives accident-reconstruction experts the chance to examine the scene and vehicles. The evidence that builds a strong case typically includes:

  • The police report and any citations issued.
  • ELD and engine-control-module data showing speed, braking, and hours driven.
  • Driver logs, qualification files, and drug-testing records.
  • Truck maintenance and inspection records.
  • Photographs and video of the scene, vehicles, and injuries.
  • Witness statements and any traffic- or dash-camera footage.

Louisiana Law and the One-Year Deadline

Louisiana follows a pure comparative fault rule under La. C.C. art. 2323, so even if you are found partly responsible, you can still recover damages reduced by your percentage of fault. Just as important is the deadline: under La. C.C. art. 3492, you generally have only one year from the date of the crash to file a personal injury lawsuit. Because truck cases require fast evidence preservation and a thorough investigation, it is wise to involve a lawyer as soon as possible rather than waiting until the deadline approaches.
