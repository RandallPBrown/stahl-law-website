Where Can I Work With a DUI in Louisiana?

By Christopher M. Stahl · Criminal Defense

A DUI conviction can feel like a roadblock to your career, but it does not have to be the end of your earning potential. Many Louisiana employers are still willing to hire qualified, reliable people who have a conviction in their past — especially in roles that do not require driving. This article walks through realistic job options after a DUI, from non-driving positions to training programs and self-employment.

Non-Driving Jobs You Can Pursue

The most straightforward path after a DUI is a job that does not involve operating a vehicle. Office roles, retail positions, and warehouse work are common examples. Popular non-driving jobs include administrative assistants, customer service representatives, and warehouse associates.

Keep in mind that some non-driving jobs may still ask for a valid driver’s license — sometimes just to verify your identity or to cover the occasional errand. If you can demonstrate that you are dependable and forthright about your record, many employers will still extend an offer.

Work-From-Home Opportunities

Remote work has expanded dramatically, and many of these roles never require you to leave the house. Customer service, data entry, and virtual-assistant positions are widely available. When you search, stick to reputable companies and well-known job boards such as Indeed or LinkedIn, and be cautious of postings that ask for money up front or seem too good to be true.

Delivery and Driving Jobs

Driving jobs are not automatically off-limits, but they depend heavily on your specific record and the employer’s insurance requirements. Some package and food-delivery companies will still consider applicants with a DUI, often after a background check. If driving is central to the work, be honest about your situation and confirm whether your license status meets the company’s policy.

Skilled Trades and Construction

The trades offer solid pay and benefits, and most positions do not hinge on a clean driving record. Carpenters, electricians, and HVAC technicians are all in demand across Northwest Louisiana. These jobs typically require a background check, so always be truthful about your conviction — misrepresenting your record can lead to termination or further legal trouble.

Freelancing and Entrepreneurship

Self-employment gives you flexibility and sidesteps the traditional hiring screen. As a freelancer or business owner, you can market your skills directly to clients. Graphic design, writing, social media management, and web development are popular freelance fields you can launch on platforms like Upwork or Fiverr. Small-business ideas such as pet grooming, cleaning services, home repair, and personal training can also let you build income on your own terms.

Government Jobs

A DUI does not necessarily disqualify you from public-sector work. Many city and state positions are non-driving roles, including administrative jobs, library assistants, clerks, and maintenance workers. Government employers routinely run background checks, so disclose your conviction and research each position’s specific requirements before you apply.

Job Training Programs in the Shreveport–Bossier City Area

If you want to build new, in-demand skills, several local and statewide programs can help:

  • Bossier Parish Community College — workforce training in healthcare, manufacturing, and transportation.
  • Northwest Louisiana Technical Community College — programs in welding, carpentry, and electrical technology.
  • Caddo Parish Adult Education — free career training and job-placement assistance, including CNA, industrial maintenance, and welding.
  • LSU Shreveport — continuing-education certificates in project management, cybersecurity, and human resources.
  • Goodwill Industries of North Louisiana — computer-skills training, customer-service training, and placement help.
  • Step Forward — a workforce program offering construction training, production-technician certification, and hospitality training.

A Few Realistic Notes

Some fields — healthcare, positions requiring a security clearance, and commercial driving regulated by the FMCSA — apply heightened scrutiny to a DUI. A conviction is rarely an automatic bar, but the offense level, how much time has passed, and your conduct since will all matter. Honesty on applications is always the better strategy; background checks are easy to run, and undisclosed convictions tend to cause far bigger problems than the conviction itself.

One of the most effective long-term steps is to fight the charge in the first place or, where eligible, pursue expungement later. Reducing or clearing a conviction can meaningfully widen the doors that are open to you.
