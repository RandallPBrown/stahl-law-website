Domestic Violence Charges in Shreveport: What You Need to Know

A domestic violence arrest can turn your world upside down in a matter of hours. Whether charges stem from a heated argument, a misunderstanding, or a situation far more complicated than the police report suggests, the consequences in Louisiana can be serious and long-lasting. If you or someone you love is facing a domestic violence charge in Shreveport or Bossier City, understanding how the law works — and acting quickly — can make a significant difference in the outcome of your case.

What Counts as Domestic Violence Under Louisiana Law?

Louisiana law broadly defines domestic abuse to include acts of physical harm, battery, assault, stalking, and certain other offenses when they occur between people in specific relationships. Those relationships typically include:

  • Current or former spouses
  • Couples who share a child
  • People who are dating or have dated
  • Household members or family members living together

It is important to understand that a charge does not require visible injuries. Law enforcement in Caddo and Bossier parishes are trained to make arrests whenever they have probable cause to believe domestic abuse has occurred — even if the alleged victim does not want to press charges. Once an arrest is made, the decision to prosecute rests with the District Attorney's office, not the alleged victim.

Potential Consequences of a Conviction

Louisiana takes domestic violence offenses seriously, and the penalties can escalate significantly depending on your history and the specific circumstances of the alleged incident. A first-offense domestic abuse battery conviction can result in jail time, fines, mandatory counseling programs, and a period of probation. Subsequent offenses carry much heavier penalties, and prior convictions can elevate charges from misdemeanors to felonies.

Beyond the criminal penalties themselves, a domestic violence conviction can affect your life in ways that go well beyond the courtroom:

  • Protective orders: A restraining or protective order may prohibit you from returning to your own home or seeing your children.
  • Firearm rights: Federal law generally prohibits individuals convicted of domestic violence misdemeanors from owning or possessing firearms — a major concern for many residents of Northwest Louisiana.
  • Child custody: A conviction or even a pending charge can be used against you in family court proceedings.
  • Employment: Many employers conduct background checks, and a domestic violence record can cost you job opportunities, professional licenses, or security clearances.
  • Immigration status: For non-citizens, a domestic violence conviction can trigger serious immigration consequences, including deportation.

Common Defenses in Domestic Violence Cases

Being charged is not the same as being convicted, and there are often legitimate defenses available. Every situation is unique, but an experienced criminal defense attorney will carefully examine the facts of your case and may explore strategies such as:

  • Self-defense or defense of others: Louisiana law recognizes the right to protect yourself or another person from harm.
  • False allegations: Domestic disputes can be emotionally charged, and accusations are sometimes exaggerated or fabricated — particularly during contentious divorces or custody battles.
  • Lack of evidence: The prosecution must prove every element of the offense beyond a reasonable doubt. Inconsistent statements, lack of physical evidence, or unreliable witnesses can all undermine the state's case.
  • Violation of your rights: If law enforcement failed to follow proper procedures during your arrest or investigation, evidence may be suppressible.

What to Do If You Have Been Arrested

The steps you take immediately after an arrest matter enormously. Here is what we strongly recommend:

  • Stay calm and do not resist. Resisting arrest will only add charges and complicate your situation.
  • Exercise your right to remain silent. Anything you say to law enforcement can and will be used against you. Politely decline to answer questions until you have spoken with an attorney.
  • Do not contact the alleged victim. Even if a protective order has not yet been formally issued, reaching out can be used against you in court.
  • Contact a defense attorney as soon as possible. Early intervention gives your attorney the best chance to gather evidence, interview witnesses, and engage with prosecutors before positions become entrenched.

Why Local Experience Matters

Navigating the criminal courts in Caddo and Bossier parishes is not like navigating courts anywhere else. Local knowledge — familiarity with the judges, prosecutors, and the way domestic violence cases are typically handled in this area — can be a genuine advantage. An attorney who regularly practices in Shreveport and Bossier City understands the local landscape and can craft a strategy tailored to your specific situation.
