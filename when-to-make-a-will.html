The Best Times in Life to Make a Will

By Christopher M. Stahl · Wills & Successions

No one enjoys thinking about their own death, so it is no surprise that writing a will is one of the most postponed tasks in adult life. Yet a clear, valid will is one of the kindest things you can do for the people you love. It tells the world where your assets should go, names who will carry out your wishes, and spares your family confusion and conflict during an already difficult time. The question most people ask is simple: when is the right time? Below are the life moments that should prompt you to write — or update — a will.

When You Turn Eighteen

In Louisiana, you must generally be at least sixteen to make a will, and reaching adulthood is a natural first opportunity to put one in place. Even a young adult with modest assets, a vehicle, or a bank account benefits from saying clearly who should receive what. Establishing the habit early makes updating it later far easier.

When You Get Married

A change in your closest relationships is a strong reason to write or revise a will. Marriage reshapes your financial picture, and Louisiana’s community property rules mean that what each spouse owns — and what passes at death — can be more complex than couples expect. Putting a will in place after marriage helps ensure your separate and community property are handled the way you intend.

When You Have Children

Few events make a will more important than having children. A will lets you provide for your children the way you want and, critically, lets you nominate a tutor (Louisiana’s term for a guardian) to care for minor children if both parents pass away. Naming someone you trust gives you peace of mind that your children will be looked after.

Louisiana parents should also understand forced heirship, a rule unique among the states. Children who are under the age of 24, or who are permanently incapable of caring for themselves, may be entitled to a portion of your estate as “forced heirs,” regardless of what your will says. Planning with this rule in mind helps you avoid surprises and keeps your wishes enforceable.

When You Buy Property

Purchasing a home or other significant asset changes the overall value of your estate and is a sound reason to write or revisit your will. A major acquisition is a good prompt to confirm that your plan still reflects who should inherit and in what proportions. If a purchase involves moving to a new state, you should also have your documents reviewed under that state’s laws.

When You Start a Business

If you own a business, your will is the place to address what happens to it. Whether you envision a family member taking over or someone else stepping in, you can spell out that succession plan and, if more than one person is to inherit, how the shares should be divided. Coordinating your will with any business agreements protects what you have built.

Every Few Years — and After Major Life Changes

Even without a single triggering event, it is wise to revisit your will periodically. Many attorneys suggest reviewing it about every five years, and also after a divorce, the death of a beneficiary, a significant change in assets, or a move. Your priorities, relationships, and the law all evolve over time, and an outdated will can create the very disputes you hoped to avoid.

Get It Done Correctly

Louisiana has specific formalities for a valid will — most commonly the notarial will, which must be signed before a notary and two competent witnesses following precise statutory requirements. A small mistake in form can render a will invalid, which is why preparing one with a qualified attorney is so valuable. Writing a will is ultimately an act of confidence and care: it ensures the welfare of your loved ones and helps prevent legal disputes over your estate once you are gone.
