Can You Be a Teacher With a DUI in Louisiana?

By Christopher M. Stahl · Criminal Defense

For most professionals, a DUI is a serious setback. For teachers, it can feel like a threat to an entire career. Educators are held to a high standard of conduct, and a DWI arrest naturally raises worries about your license, your current job, and your prospects in any future district. The good news is that a DUI is not automatically a career-ender — but the stakes are real, and how the case is handled matters enormously.

Can You Lose Your Teaching License?

Losing your license is possible, but it is far from the only outcome. The result depends on your circumstances, your employment status, and how the charge is ultimately resolved.

If you are not currently employed as an educator but hold a license, a conviction can make it harder to land a position, and your credential could be reviewed by the state licensing authority. Successfully fighting the charge — or reducing it — can improve your odds of being hired by a school district.

Experienced and tenured teachers tend to have more protection. Suspension or termination is possible, but many keep their licenses, especially for a first offense without aggravating facts. Finding a new teaching position may take effort, but it is rarely impossible.

Public vs. Private Schools

Where you teach makes a difference. Public-school teachers in Louisiana generally enjoy more procedural protections — including due-process rights tied to tenure — which makes it more likely you can keep your position after a DUI. Private schools, by contrast, often operate under at-will employment and may reserve the right to terminate a teacher without giving a reason. Reviewing your contract and any district or school policy is an important early step.

What Factors Affect Your Employment

DWI cases are not one-size-fits-all, and several details can heavily influence whether a teacher keeps a job or faces license action:

  • Whether anyone was hurt. If the DWI led to an accident that injured another person, termination becomes far more likely than if no one was harmed.
  • The severity of the charge. Aggravating circumstances — such as injury to others — can elevate the offense. A felony-level outcome is especially damaging because schools and employers will see it on a background check.
  • Prior offenses. A repeat DWI raises the risk to both your job and your driver’s license.
  • A high BAC. An especially high blood alcohol concentration can increase penalties and the likelihood of professional consequences.

One reality teachers should not ignore: background checks are easy to run, and a conviction cannot simply be hidden. That is exactly why early, strategic defense is so important.

Reporting and Licensing Considerations

Teachers should be aware that certain charges or convictions may trigger reporting obligations to a school district or to the state licensing authority, and contracts often contain morality or conduct clauses. Before making any statement to an employer, it is wise to understand your obligations and your rights. An attorney can help you navigate disclosure questions without unnecessarily harming your case or your career.

Why a Strong Defense Matters

Because the difference between a dismissed charge, a reduced charge, and a conviction can determine whether you keep your license and your livelihood, a teacher facing a DWI should treat the case as the high-stakes matter it is. A defense attorney can examine the legality of the traffic stop, the administration of field sobriety and chemical tests, and whether your constitutional rights were respected — any of which may provide grounds to challenge or reduce the charge. Protecting the outcome of the criminal case is the most direct way to protect your career.
