Adoption Attorney in Bossier City & Shreveport

Guiding Northwest Louisiana families through the legal steps of welcoming a child.

Adoption is one of the most rewarding steps a family can take, but the legal process behind it is detailed and exacting. Louisiana law sets specific requirements for who may adopt, how consent is given, and how a court finalizes the new parent-child relationship. Christopher Stahl helps families across Bossier City and Shreveport handle that paperwork and procedure so they can focus on the joy of bringing a child into their home. Whether you are a stepparent, a relative, or a couple working with an agency, having clear guidance can keep the process moving and reduce the chance of costly delays.

Types of Adoption in Louisiana

Louisiana recognizes several routes to adoption, and the steps you follow depend on which one fits your situation.

Stepparent Adoption

One of the most common adoptions, a stepparent adoption lets a spouse become the legal parent of their husband’s or wife’s child. Because adoption ends the parental rights of the child’s other legal parent, that parent’s consent is usually required, unless their rights have already been terminated or the law allows the court to proceed without it in certain circumstances. Stepparent adoptions are often more streamlined than other types and may not require the same home study that agency placements do.

Intrafamily Adoption

When a grandparent, aunt, uncle, sibling, or other close relative adopts a child already in their care, Louisiana treats it as an intrafamily adoption. These cases frequently arise when a relative has been raising a child and wishes to make that arrangement permanent. Like stepparent adoptions, they tend to involve a more direct path through the court.

Agency Adoption

In an agency adoption, a licensed public or private agency takes custody of a child and places the child with the adoptive family. The agency typically handles the surrender of parental rights, screening of prospective parents, and much of the coordination. Families adopting through the state’s foster care system fall within this category.

Private Adoption

A private, or independent, adoption is arranged directly between a birth parent and the adoptive family, often with each side represented by counsel. These adoptions require careful attention to consent and to the legal requirements that protect everyone involved, including the birth parents’ rights and any applicable waiting periods.

Consent and Parental Rights

Almost every adoption turns on consent. Before a child can be adopted, the legal rights of the existing parents generally must be voluntarily surrendered or terminated by a court. Louisiana law sets out who must consent, the form that consent must take, and the limited situations in which a court may proceed without a parent’s agreement, such as abandonment or a prior termination of rights. Because these rules are strict and the consequences are permanent, this is an area where experienced guidance matters.

The Home Study

Many adoptions require a home study, an assessment by a qualified evaluator that looks at the prospective parents’ home, background, and readiness to raise a child. The study commonly includes interviews, background and criminal-history checks, and a visit to the home. Stepparent and some intrafamily adoptions may be exempt or subject to a reduced review, while agency and private placements usually require a full study. The goal is simply to confirm that the placement serves the child’s best interests.

Finalizing the Adoption

The process ends with a finalization hearing, where a judge reviews the case and, if everything is in order, signs a final decree of adoption. From that point, the adoptive parents have all the rights and responsibilities of any parent, and the child gains full legal status in the family, including inheritance rights. An amended birth certificate reflecting the adoptive parents can then be issued. Many families describe the finalization hearing as the moment their long process becomes real.

How an Attorney Helps

Even an uncontested adoption involves petitions, consents, notices, and a court appearance that must each be handled correctly. An attorney can confirm which type of adoption applies, prepare and file the paperwork, address consent and any termination of parental rights, coordinate the home study where required, and represent your family at the finalization hearing. Getting these details right from the start helps your adoption proceed as smoothly as possible.
