What Does the Law Say About Personal Injury?

Many injuries caused by others are unintentional — but a lack of intent doesn’t remove responsibility. If you’re injured in an accident, you may bring a claim of negligence. Car, pedestrian, and commercial-truck accidents, slips and falls, and injuries from poor building maintenance all fall under negligence: the at-fault party failed to protect the injured person, and that failure gives rise to a right to compensation. These claims succeed when the defendant owed a duty of care to the injured person.

Why Is Duty of Care Crucial?

Storekeepers, business owners, and drivers all have a duty to protect others — to keep visitors safe and to drive safely. Failing that duty and injuring a visitor, pedestrian, motorist, or passenger gives the injured person the right to seek compensation. Intentional, criminal acts are an exception handled separately: when a crime such as battery causes injury, the harmed person generally must pursue compensation through a civil lawsuit, since criminal courts can’t order a defendant to pay the victim — which is exactly why experience in both areas matters.

How Can a Personal Injury Lawyer Help?

An experienced attorney knows how courts weigh negligence and the duty of care owed. Louisiana follows a pure comparative negligence rule: if you’re found partially at fault, your award is reduced by your share. Sought $5,000 and found 50% at fault? You can recover $2,500. A lawyer also handles strict-liability situations — defective products, unremedied hazards, and certain dog-bite injuries — so the right parts of the law are applied to your case.

Filing a Claim — Time Limits Matter

You have only a limited time to file. Under Louisiana Civil Code art. 3492, you generally have one year from the date of a negligence-based injury to file. Claims arising from a crime (such as aggravated battery or assault) carry a two-year prescription, and certain sexual-assault claims have a longer period. Because time limits vary with the act and the victim’s age, it’s important to speak with a lawyer promptly.

How Much Is Your Claim Worth?

Value depends on the number and extent of your damages, which may include:

  • Medical expenses — past, current, and future treatment, therapy, equipment, and care.
  • Lost wages — for work missed due to treatment or inability to perform job tasks.
  • Property damage — repair or replacement, common in product and traffic cases.
  • Other expenses — services you must now hire out.
  • Non-economic damages — pain, anxiety, disfigurement, and loss of companionship.
  • Wrongful death — funeral costs, lost benefits and wages, and loss of guidance and support.

Most personal injury cases have no cap on recovery; medical-malpractice claims in Louisiana are subject to a $500,000 cap (in addition to future medical care). Under La. C.C. art. 2323’s comparative fault, a $100,000 award reduced by 40% fault yields $60,000.
